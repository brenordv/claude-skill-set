# Pitch deck

A 12-slide deck outline with per-slide content. Structural rules: one message per slide (needing
two messages means needing two slides), real numbers everywhere ("$0" is better than hiding
behind "pre-revenue"), the deck tells one story (problem, solution, proof, opportunity, team,
ask), and the strongest material goes early because initial screens are fast, often minutes.

Input needed: the startup, what is being raised, and current metrics.

## Per slide

Give each slide a title, the one takeaway an investor should keep, the content (bullets with the
specific data to include), and a design note (which visual or chart).

1. **Title.** Company name, one-line description, founder name and title. The description passes
   the "told at a party" test: a non-technical stranger gets it in 5 seconds.
2. **Problem.** A quantified pain point, who has it and how many of them, and what they do today
   (and why that fails them).
3. **Solution.** What the product does in one sentence, 3 capabilities that map onto the problem,
   and a product visual.
4. **Demo / product.** The core flow in 3-4 steps, the moment where the user gets unexpected
   value, a before/after if one exists.
5. **Market size.** TAM/SAM/SOM built bottom-up with sources, never just "the market is $50B",
   plus the "why now": what changed to make this possible or necessary today.
6. **Traction.** Users, revenue, growth rate, retention; pre-revenue substitutes are waitlist,
   LOIs, pilots, engagement. Name exactly what the up-and-to-the-right chart plots.
7. **Business model.** How the money comes in with actual pricing, unit economics (CAC, LTV,
   ratio, gross margin), and 12-24 month projections with assumptions stated.
8. **Competition.** A 2x2 with axes deliberately chosen where this product wins, why the position
   is defensible, and the honest statement of what no competitor does.
9. **Go-to-market.** The primary acquisition channel with estimated CAC, what works now, and what
   the funding accelerates.
10. **Team.** Why this team wins this market: specific achievements, not job titles; the hires
    this round funds.
11. **The ask.** Amount, use of funds in 3-4 buckets with percentages, the milestones the capital
    reaches, and the target timing of the next round.
12. **Closing.** The vision in one bold sentence, contact details, and one data point that
    sticks.

## Appendix

Recommend 3-5 backup slides for Q&A: the detailed financial model, technical architecture where
relevant, customer quotes or case studies, and the deeper competitive breakdown.
