# Landing page

Write complete, conversion-focused copy for every section of a landing page. Copy rules: address
the reader ("you" beats "we" beats "our product"), every headline must survive being the only
thing a visitor reads, no jargon the audience does not use daily, and concrete numbers beat
adjectives ("saves 4 hours a week" beats "saves time"). Delete filler words: very, really, just,
simply. House prose rules (`brain/knowledge/writing-style.md`) apply on top.

Input needed: the product, target audience, and value proposition. Stage matters for the social
proof section.

## Sections

**1. Hero.** Headline (6-12 words, a clear outcome, no wordplay; a stranger gets it in 5
seconds). Subheadline (15-25 words: who it is for and how it works). CTA button text that names
the value ("Start analyzing competitors", never "Get Started"). One social-proof line under the
CTA, drawn from real numbers the founder actually has.

**2. Problem.** A section headline naming the pain in the customer's own words, then 3 pain
points of 1-2 sentences each, written as the reader would say them, with the load-bearing phrase
bolded.

**3. Solution.** A headline bridging problem to product, then 3 benefit blocks: short title (3-5
words), 2-3 sentences focused on outcome rather than features, and one concrete detail (a number,
a timeframe, a comparison).

**4. How it works.** 3-4 numbered steps from signup to value, one sentence each; the whole flow
should feel doable in 5 minutes.

**5. Social proof.** Match the type to the stage: pre-launch uses waitlist count, advisor quotes,
or team credibility; early stage uses beta-user quotes and first-user numbers; growing uses a
logo bar, case studies, and specific figures. Draft 2 placeholder testimonials to show shape and
tone, clearly marked `[PLACEHOLDER - replace with a real quote]`; fabricated testimonials must
never ship.

**6. Pricing preview (optional).** Simple pricing gets a brief section; complex pricing gets
"starting at $X/month" with a link to the full page. Either way, one line that answers "is it
worth it?".

**7. FAQ.** 5 questions answering the top objections, 2-3 sentences each, at least one on data
security or privacy. Each answer turns the objection into a reason to sign up.

**8. Final CTA.** A headline reinforcing the transformation, the same CTA as the hero (or a
variant), and a risk reversal: free trial, money-back guarantee, or "no credit card required",
whichever is true for this product.

**9. SEO metadata.** Title tag (50-60 characters), meta description (150-160 characters), and 3
target keywords.
