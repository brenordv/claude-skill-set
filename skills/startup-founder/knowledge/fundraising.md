# Fundraising

Assess whether the founder should raise at all, then size the round, build the investor pipeline,
and plan the process. Calibrate everything to the actual stage, and remember investors will be
skeptical; prepare the founder for skepticism instead of cheering. Valuation and terms data ages
fast: verify current stage norms when the runtime has web access, and label remembered ranges
with the period they come from. Instrument and term decisions get reviewed by a lawyer before
signature; this file is strategy, not legal advice.

Input needed: stage, metrics, and target raise amount.

## 1. Readiness assessment

Score 1-5 per dimension, with the founder's specific gap named:

| Dimension      | What investors look for at each stage                                        |
|----------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Traction       | Pre-seed: idea + team. Seed: early users or revenue. Series A: PMF + growth  |
| Team           | Full-time commitment, technical co-founder, relevant domain experience        |
| Market         | Large enough for venture returns, growing, timed right                        |
| Product        | Working MVP, user feedback, retention data                                    |
| Unit economics | CAC, LTV, gross margin, even as rough estimates                               |
| Story          | Explainable in 30 seconds in a way that makes a stranger care                 |

Verdict: ready to raise / needs 1-3 months (with the exact gap list) / too early (with the plan
to get ready). When the verdict is "not ready", that is the deliverable; do not soften it.

## 2. Round sizing and terms

The recommended amount (what makes sense, not what the founder hoped), the instrument (SAFE,
convertible note, or priced round) with the reasoning, a valuation range grounded in current
market comparables for the stage, the runway the money must buy in months, and the dilution the
founder should expect.

## 3. Investor targeting

Profile who to target: fund stage, check size, sector focus, geography, hands-on vs. passive.
Then a tiered pipeline of at least 50 names:

- Tier 1, the 10 dream investors, met last with the sharpest pitch.
- Tier 2, 20 strong fits.
- Tier 3, 20 backups, met first as practice rounds.

Where to find them: investor databases (Crunchbase, OpenVC, Signal by NFX and similar; confirm
which are currently useful), relevant investors' public writing and feeds, and concrete warm-intro
tactics rather than "network more".

## 4. Materials checklist

| Material                  | State needed | Priority     |
|---------------------------|--------------|--------------|
| Pitch deck (10-12 slides) | Polished     | Must have    |
| One-pager                 | Polished     | Must have    |
| Product demo              | Working      | Must have    |
| Cap table                 | Clean        | Must have    |
| Financial model (24 mo)   | Draft        | Should have  |
| Data room                 | Organized    | Should have  |
| Customer references       | 2-3 ready    | Nice to have |

Build the deck per `pitch-deck.md` and the metrics story per `metrics.md`.

## 5. Timeline

A 12-week process, run as a batch so term sheets land close together: weeks 1-2 preparation
(materials, target list, intro requests); 3-4 first meetings with tier 3; 5-6 tier 2 with the
refined pitch; 7-8 tier 1 with momentum and social proof; 9-10 follow-ups, partner meetings,
diligence; 11-12 term-sheet negotiation and close.
