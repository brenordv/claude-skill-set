# Competitor analysis

Map the market, compare features, and find the positioning gap worth owning. This deliverable
lives or dies on data freshness: pricing pages, funding news, and changelogs move monthly, so
verify with a live search when the runtime allows it, and label anything from memory as
possibly stale.

Input needed: the product or market to analyze, and what the founder is building if a comparison
column is wanted.

## 1. Market landscape

Identify 5-8 direct and indirect competitors. For each: name and URL, founded year and funding
stage (bootstrapped, seed, Series A, ...), pricing model with actual prices where findable,
target segment (enterprise, SMB, prosumer, consumer), and the one-sentence differentiator they
would put on their own homepage.

## 2. Feature comparison matrix

A markdown table: competitors as columns, 8-12 features that matter in this market as rows, cells
holding Yes / No / Partial. Add a column for the user's product marked "Planned" or "Building".
Pick features buyers actually compare on, not features vendors brag about.

## 3. Positioning gaps

2-3 gaps no competitor fully covers. For each: what is missing, why users care, build difficulty
(low/medium/high), and a rough estimate of the head start the founder gets by filling it first.

## 4. Threat assessment

Rank the top 3 competitors by threat (high/medium/low) using resource advantage (funding, team
size), feature overlap with the user's product, and iteration speed (their changelog or release
history is the evidence; check it rather than assuming).

## 5. Recommendations

- The position to own: one niche to dominate before expanding.
- The feature to ship first: the single feature that creates the most differentiation.
- The competitor to watch: who is most likely to enter this exact niche next, and why.

## Rules

- "They raised $5M in January" beats "they have funding"; specificity is the whole product here.
- Missing data is reported as missing, never papered over with a guess.
